## 1. Purpose

This document explains how findings from the competitor research inform proposed features and design decisions for Thrill Pass.

The purpose is not to copy existing platforms but to examine useful approaches and determine which ideas may support our proposed adventure-discovery and booking service.

## 2. Research Evidence and Proposed Decisions

### Finding 1: Search and activity discovery

**Reference:** GetYourGuide

**Evidence:** During the November 15 website inspection, the homepage displayed a search bar, destination cards, and sections for discovering attractions and experiences.

**Interpretation:** Search and organized activity discovery may help visitors identify experiences that interest them.

**Proposed Thrill Pass decision:** Investigate a homepage search function and recognizable adventure categories, including ATV rides, ziplining, and escape rooms.

**Planned reviewer check:** Ask a reviewer to locate an adventure activity using the proposed search and category organization.

### Finding 2: Destination and date selection

**Reference:** Viator

**Evidence:** During the November 15 inspection, the homepage displayed separate fields for searching by destination or activity and selecting dates.

**Interpretation:** Location and date are potentially useful criteria when customers explore activities.

**Proposed Thrill Pass decision:** Investigate location and date filters for adventure discovery.

**Planned reviewer check:** Ask a reviewer to find an activity in New Jersey or nearby New York and identify how available dates would be presented.

### Finding 3: Local categories and promotional information

**Reference:** Groupon

**Evidence:** During the November 15 inspection, the local deals page displayed activity categories, location selection, filters, ratings, and promotional prices.

**Interpretation:** Category organization and visible pricing may help visitors compare local experiences.

**Proposed Thrill Pass decision:** Investigate activity categories, clear price presentation, and relevant filters.

**Planned reviewer check:** Ask a reviewer to compare two proposed adventure listings using their displayed prices and activity information.

## 3. Comparison of Design Alternatives

### Alternative A: General adventure marketplace

This approach would provide a broad selection of recreational and entertainment activities.

**Potential advantage:** It could accommodate different customer interests.

**Potential limitation:** A broad selection may make it harder to establish a clear initial project focus.

### Alternative B: Focused adventure-discovery platform

This approach would initially emphasize ATV rides, ziplining, escape rooms, and related adventure experiences in New Jersey and nearby New York.

**Potential advantage:** It provides a clearer initial business concept and a manageable research scope.

**Potential limitation:** A narrower selection may exclude customers interested in other recreational activities.

### Current proposed direction

The team will investigate Alternative B as its initial direction, with the possibility of expanding the activity categories later.

This is a preliminary team direction, not a conclusion established through customer interviews.

## 4. Proposed Core Customer Workflow

The proposed customer workflow is:

1. Visit the Thrill Pass homepage.
2. Search for an adventure or browse activity categories.
3. Select a preferred location.
4. Compare relevant experiences and available information.
5. Review prices, requirements, and booking conditions.
6. Proceed to the booking process where a verified booking option exists.

This workflow is a design proposal and has not yet been tested with customers.

## 5. Research-to-Design Decision Summary

| Research reference | Proposed design direction |
|---|---|
| GetYourGuide | Search and activity categories |
| Viator | Location and date selection |
| Groupon | Local discovery, filters, and price presentation |

These decisions are based on observed website interfaces and published platform information. They do not establish that customers have requested these features.

## 6. Verification Plan

The team proposes the following checks:

- Can a reviewer locate an ATV experience?
- Can a reviewer identify an activity's location?
- Can a reviewer compare two activities?
- Can a reviewer locate price and booking information?
- Can a reviewer understand the steps required to proceed with a booking?

**Verification status:** Planned, not yet performed.

After a reviewer completes the checks, the team will record the actual feedback, resulting decisions, changes, and verification evidence.

## 8. Research Limitations

The current decisions are based on competitor website inspections and published information.

The team has not yet established actual customer preferences through interviews or usability testing.

Customer personas and statements developed for planning must be identified as hypotheses or simulations, not actual customer observations.

Further research and peer feedback may result in changes to the proposed features.
