## 1. Purpose of the Comparison

The purpose of this research is to examine three existing digital platforms that help customers discover, compare, purchase, or book activities and experiences.

The platforms selected are:

1. GetYourGuide
2. Viator
3. Groupon

GetYourGuide and Viator are direct references because they provide marketplaces for tours, activities, and experiences. Groupon is an adjacent reference because it helps customers discover local activities and purchase promotional offers.

The comparison will help the Thrill Pass team understand existing customer workflows, identify useful features, and consider opportunities for a more focused adventure-discovery experience.

## 2. Reference One: GetYourGuide

**Website:** https://www.getyourguide.com/

**Original supporting source:** https://www.getyourguide.press/blog/springrelease2026

**Source publication date:** April 22, 2026  
**Date accessed:** November 15, 2026

### Documented features

GetYourGuide provides a marketplace where customers can search for destinations and activities, browse experiences, read verified traveler reviews, and book activities.

Its published information describes:

- Search and discovery of activities.
- Verified customer reviews.
- Booking and ticket management through its app.
- Instant booking confirmation for available experiences.
- Flexible cancellation on many bookings.
- Customer support.
- Activity recommendations and search improvements.

### Strengths identified

The platform combines activity discovery, customer reviews, booking, and access to booking information.

Its review and cancellation information can help customers evaluate an experience before making a booking.

### Possible limitations and research opportunities

GetYourGuide serves a broad international travel market. Our research does not establish that it is specifically designed around independent local adventure providers in New Jersey and nearby New York.

A possible opportunity for Thrill Pass is to focus its initial research and proposed experience on a narrower geographical area and adventure categories such as ATV rides, ziplining, and escape rooms.

This is an opportunity hypothesis, not a verified weakness in GetYourGuide.

## 3. Reference Two: Viator

**Website:** https://www.viator.com/

**Original supporting source:** https://www.viator.com/

**Date accessed:** November 15, 2026

### Documented features

Viator provides a marketplace for tours, activities, and travel experiences.

Its published homepage identifies:

- Search for destinations and activities.
- A large selection of experiences.
- Customer reviews.
- Twenty-four-hour customer support.
- Flexible booking options.
- Reserve-now-and-pay-later options.
- Free cancellation on many experiences when the stated conditions are met.

### Strengths identified

Viator emphasizes customer confidence through reviews, flexible booking arrangements, and customer support.

These features are relevant to Thrill Pass because customers may want information about an adventure before committing to a booking.

### Possible limitations and research opportunities

Viator serves a broad range of travel and tourism experiences.

Thrill Pass could investigate whether a more focused presentation of adventure activities and local providers would make it easier for its intended audience to find relevant experiences.

This is a proposed direction for investigation, not evidence that Viator's existing search system is ineffective.

## 4. Reference Three: Groupon

**Website:** https://www.groupon.com/local

**Original supporting sources:**

- https://www.groupon.com/faq/getting-started-with-groupon/welcome-to-groupon
- https://www.groupon.com/faq/getting-started-with-groupon/buying-groupons

**Date accessed:** November 15, 2026

### Documented features

Groupon helps customers discover local deals and experiences.

Its published information identifies:

- Location-based searches.
- Categories for local activities and experiences.
- Search filters.
- Customer ratings and reviews.
- Promotional offers.
- Deal descriptions and redemption instructions.
- Restrictions and conditions associated with individual offers.

Its local activities include examples such as escape rooms, mini golf, boat tours, and other recreational experiences.

### Strengths identified

Groupon combines local discovery with promotional offers.

Its deal descriptions and conditions provide information that customers should examine before purchasing an experience.

### Possible limitations and research opportunities

Groupon's local marketplace covers many business categories, not only adventure activities.

Thrill Pass could explore a more specialized presentation focused on adventure experiences, with activity requirements, locations, available dates, and booking information made easy to find.

This is a potential design opportunity rather than a verified customer complaint.

## 5. Comparison of the Three Platforms

| Comparison area | GetYourGuide | Viator | Groupon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main focus | Tours and experiences | Tours and activities | Local deals and experiences |
| Discovery | Search and recommendations | Search and destinations | Location and category searches |
| Customer reviews | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Booking or purchase | Activity bookings | Activity bookings | Deal purchases and redemption |
| Flexible booking | Available on many experiences | Available on many experiences | Depends on individual deal |
| Local discovery | Destination-based | Destination-based | Strong local-deal orientation |

### Comparison findings

The three platforms demonstrate different approaches to helping customers discover and purchase experiences.

GetYourGuide and Viator emphasize activity discovery, reviews, and booking flexibility.

Groupon emphasizes local discovery, promotional offers, and deal conditions.

An important shared lesson is that customers need useful information before making a purchasing or booking decision.

## 6. What Thrill Pass Could Learn

Based on the documented features, the team proposes investigating the following opportunities:

1. **Focused activity categories:** Organize ATV rides, ziplining, escape rooms, and other adventures into recognizable categories.

2. **Location-based discovery:** Help customers find activities in New Jersey and nearby New York.

3. **Useful activity information:** Present prices, descriptions, locations, age requirements, safety restrictions, and other relevant conditions where verified information is available.

4. **Customer confidence:** Consider displaying provider information and genuine customer reviews when available.

5. **Booking convenience:** Make the steps for selecting an activity and requesting or completing a booking clear.

These are proposed features. Their usefulness has not yet been established through customer interviews or usability testing.

## 8. Research Limitations

This comparison uses published information from the three platforms.

The research does not establish:

- That customers in New Jersey experience the same difficulties.
- That existing platforms fail to meet local customer needs.
- That adventure providers want to join Thrill Pass.
- That the proposed features will increase bookings.
- That all listed activities are currently available in our target area.

Further investigation is necessary before making these claims.

The team should also check actual local provider availability, activity requirements, and booking arrangements before developing location-specific examples.

## 9. Conclusion

The comparison demonstrates that existing platforms use search, categories, reviews, location information, and booking features to help customers discover experiences.

Thrill Pass will investigate a more focused adventure-discovery concept serving New Jersey and nearby New York.

Rather than attempting to reproduce every feature offered by large marketplaces, the initial project will explore a manageable customer workflow centered on discovering, comparing, and booking adventure experiences.

The final design decisions will be informed by the combined research contributions of both group members and the feedback received through peer review.
